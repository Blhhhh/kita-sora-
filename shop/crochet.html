The Crochet Universe

Some journeys don't begin with a suitcase.
Some begin with a ball of yarn.

My mother picked up crochet sometime last year. At first, none of us thought it would last. She's always been the kind of person who gets curious about something, throws herself into it for a while, and then quietly moves on to the next interest.

But crochet stayed. Little by little, our home filled with baskets of yarn, new hooks, handwritten notebooks full of stitches, and tiny creatures waiting to be finished. Somewhere along the way, it stopped being a hobby. It simply became part of everyday life.

When I started building Kita-Sora, I jokingly told her — one day, people around the world will know your little creations. Neither of us really believed it.

But somewhere between building this website, meeting people from different countries, and sharing pieces of my own journey, something unexpected happened. People began asking about her work. Not because it was perfect. Not because it followed trends. But because it carried something difficult to imitate.

It felt like home.

Today, she still spends her afternoons crocheting quietly, sometimes following patterns, sometimes changing them halfway through, adding little ideas of her own. Every finished piece carries traces of those ordinary days.

If one of them happens to find its way into someone else's home and brings a little warmth, comfort, or joy along with it — I think that's a beautiful journey for a ball of yarn to take.

— Lynn
